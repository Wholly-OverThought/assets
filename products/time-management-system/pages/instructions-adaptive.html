Time Management System · Adaptive Edition · Draft

How This System Works

For chronic pain, limited mobility, or a body that doesn't run on a fixed schedule.

Before Anything Else Everything here — the Blocks, the tiers, the check-ins, all of it — is optional. Use what helps. Leave what doesn't. This is a toolset, not an obligation.

This system is built out of Blocks — small groups of similar or related tasks (Hygiene, Body, Doctors & Diagnoses, Relationships, and whatever else you add). You arrange Blocks into your day in the order that makes sense. When the day goes sideways — and with a variable-capacity body, it often will — you move Blocks around instead of abandoning them. A moved Block is not a missed Block.

Neuroscience, psychology, and older sources of wisdom — the Bible among them — agree on more than you'd expect about how the mind works, which is different than the brain. One point they converge on: grouping related things together makes each one easier to remember and easier to actually do. That's the whole mechanism behind a Block. But the point of this system isn't the Blocks themselves — it's using them to find your optimal routine, on whatever kind of day you're having. Flexibility isn't a workaround here. It's the entire point.

Finding that routine isn't a one-time setup, either. Any time your circumstances genuinely change — a new job with different hours, someone moving into your home, kids out of school and around all day — the steady routine you had stops working, not because you failed it, but because it was built for a life you don't have right now. Tweak it or overhaul it, depending on how big the change is — the same way you'd drop a tier on a harder day. And if no two days really look alike for you, there may be no “normal” to return to at all. This was never meant to be a routine you set once. It's a routine-builder you use as often as you need it, however often that is.

Where This Came From I built the first version of this in prison, with eighteen to twenty-two hours a day and nothing in them. Writing down tiny lists — brush teeth, shave, shower — turned out to be the only thing that got me through. I have ADHD, I'm autistic, and I live with complex PTSD — though none of it had a name yet back in prison. Grouping small, concrete tasks together is still how I keep my own days from falling apart. ADHD, for me, isn't a willpower problem — it's closer to a brain that treats anything boring as a threat and won't be reasoned with about it. Small Blocks lower the cost of just starting. The flexibility exists because a schedule that can't survive one bad hour was never going to survive me. I also live with chronic pain from a shoulder replacement that's never fully worked, and it's kept me out of most jobs — without a job's built-in structure, managing time gets harder, not easier. This edition exists because all of that is doubly true when your body doesn't cooperate either. Built to cut the shame of not finishing everything, not add to it.
The Core Idea It's not specificity that makes this work — “brush teeth,” not “hygiene,” was never really the point. The actual mechanism has three parts, all built around how an ADHD or autistic brain tends to work. First: if planning is hard — you forget, or avoid it — grouping similar tasks means you're only ever scheduling a handful of Blocks, not untangling your whole day, and those same Blocks are easy to move when plans change. Second: if a task feels too big to start, so you avoid it and then feel guilty, breaking it into small, concrete pieces removes the dread — there's no large thing left to avoid. Third: transitions are hard. Grouping similar tasks together means finishing one carries momentum into the next — it's the jump to something unrelated that creates the kind of transition most people's schedules don't account for, and that's exactly where avoidance and shame creep in. Put together: Blocks make the plan easier to build, easier to execute, and flexible enough that when something changes, the change is already part of the system — not proof you failed it.
The Core Idea, Adapted Three of the Blocks in this edition (Hygiene, Body, Doctors & Diagnoses) are tiered by capacity, not fixed by calendar. Each has a Tier 1 (flare/low-capacity), Tier 2 (moderate), and Tier 3 (higher-capacity) version. Pick whichever tier matches your body today. Completing any tier finishes the Block — there is no lesser version of done.

1Set Your Blocks

Open the Blocks page. Everything filled in — Hygiene, Body, Doctors & Diagnoses, and the rest — is an example, not a prescription. Hygiene, Body, and Doctors & Diagnoses each have a full tiered version on their own page too. Keep what fits, change what doesn't, or start fresh on the Blank Blocks pages.

2Lay Out Your Day

Each morning, use the Daily Organizer page to write your Blocks into the order you intend to run them — and note which tier you're aiming for on the tiered Blocks. It's a plan, not a contract.

3Work the Blocks, Check In

Hourly — Prayer / Meditation

A 1–5 minute pause, roughly once an hour. Check or color in the next circle in the row on the Daily Organizer — one per hour, in order.

Every 15–30 Min — Mindfulness Alarm

A short reset: where am I, what am I doing, what does my body need right now? Check or color in the next box in the grid, in sequence.

4When Your Capacity Changes

Drop a tier, don't drop the Block. Moving from a Tier 3 day to a Tier 1 day mid-day is not falling behind — it's the system working as intended. The goal is to finish your Blocks, at whatever size the day allows.

Most Important If you miss something, write it on the Skip Block so it doesn't just vanish — then let it go. This is a structured guide, not a rulebook. You will miss things. That's expected, not a failure — especially here.

5Morning & Evening

Short check-ins bookend the day on the Check-In page. (A full guided journal is a separate companion product, in development — this is just the light version that keeps the day anchored.)

6Going Portable

Two pages in this pack — Daily Organizer — Foldable and Blocks — Blank (Foldable) — are built to print on the front and back of one sheet. Print them double-sided, fold once down the center, and you've got the barebones system small enough to carry without the rest of the packet.
